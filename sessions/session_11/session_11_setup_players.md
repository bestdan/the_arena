The vignette below works whether the party came out triumphant, mauled, or short a member. If someone died, or if a new gladiator is joining the roster, this is where they walk in.

### Three Days Before the Match

The pens smell of wet stone. Whatever the children's game did to you, the arena has moved on from it already — there are bills to settle and a new spectacle to sell, and nobody in Tarsus has the attention span for last week's miracle.

What's changed is the visitors. Grey-robed academy people have been in and out of the lower galleries all week with measuring rods and ledgers, and none of them will meet a gladiator's eye. One of them was overheard complaining that the pens were "an unhygienic sample pool."

### The Night Before the Match

Snak comes into the pen late, kicks the door shut behind her, and drops onto the bench without being invited.

> "Right. Tomorrow's not a fight. Tomorrow's an *experiment*, and you're the rats."

She scrubs a hand over her face.

> "The magic colleges bought the floor. Paid the arena more than you'll see in your lives for the privilege. There's three crystal pillars going up out there right now, tall as a man and a half, and what they do is turn things into other things. Animals, mostly. Bears, snakes, whatever's in the barrel that day."

> "Before you ask — no, I'm not joking, and no, you can't opt out."

She holds up a finger.

> "Here's what matters, so listen. The pillars don't go off whenever they feel like it. They sing every third round, regular as a bell, and the announcer'll be counting it down for the crowd because that's the whole show. If you're standing close when they sing, it takes you. If you're not, it doesn't. That's the entire trick. Stand where you want to stand, and know what it costs."

> "Second thing." She leans forward. "There's a mage on the floor with them. Academy man. He does the same thing the pillars do, but by hand, and he *holds onto it* — like a man holding a rope taut. You hurt him, the rope slips. Hurt him enough and every one of his little tricks comes undone at once. If one of you is wearing fur and hating it, don't go for the fur. Go for the mage."

> "Third." She looks around at each of them. "Being an animal isn't being dead. I watched a man take the whole bout as a boar once, and he won it, and the crowd carried him out on their shoulders. You'll still be in there. You'll still be able to work the crowd — they love it more, not less. What you *won't* have is your prayers or your steel, so if you feel the change coming, spend what you've got before it lands."

She stands, stretches her back until it cracks.

> "The mage they've sent is called Corr. Ilvane Corr. Word from the gallery is he talks the whole way through and doesn't much care who's listening. Also that he's soft — never been hit in his life, that one. Make an impression."

At the door, she pauses.

> "Oh. The pillars are worth two thousand gold apiece and the academy was very clear that they're not to be damaged."

She lets that sit exactly long enough.

> "Shop's open. Some of it's for tomorrow specifically — I asked around."

### An Hour Before the Match, in the Armory

If anyone rented something from Snak that needs getting used to, this is when they get it — and this is when the academy's own people come through to take measurements. Height, weight, reach, a hand pressed flat to a cold glass plate. None of them explain why. One of them writes a number on the back of each gladiator's hand in grease pencil.

Father Crow's is **three**.

### As You Walk Into the Arena

The floor has been sunk into a shallow pit, eighty feet across, its sand raked into three pale rings of chalk. At the twelve, four and eight o'clock positions stand three crystal pillars, taller than a man, and the sound coming off them sits somewhere behind your back teeth. Bronze statues of champions long dead ring the outer edge, and between them, slabs of quarried stone throw hard shadows.

Through the south gate come three men in mismatched armor who look like they'd rather be anywhere else, a lean grey hound whose coat shifts colour as it pads across the sand, and behind them, unhurried, a slight man in academy grey with ink-stained cuffs and a wax tablet in one hand.

He does not look at the crowd. He looks at you, and writes something down.

```
"CITIZENS! The academies have paid HANDSOMELY for your attention!"

[Three crystal pillars flare in sequence — twelve, four, and eight]

"Welcome to the TRANSFORMATION TRIAL! Flesh is a SUGGESTION! Bone is
a THEORY! And our gladiators are the EXPERIMENT!"

[The crowd howls]

"Every third round, the Fonts SING! Stand too close and the shimmer
takes you! You may leave this arena a HERO — or a HOUSEPET!"

[The grey man does not look up from his tablet]

"Place your wagers, Tarsus! Not on who WINS — on what they BECOME!"
```
