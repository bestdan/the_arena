# Session 10 Setup: Kiddie Chaos

### Three Days Before the Match

The bills go up across Tarsus before anyone tells you anything. Same bill every year, same three blank circles at the bottom where the names get inked once the drum is turned.

**KIDDIE CHAOS** — and underneath, smaller: *by public lottery, as sworn before the Senate.*

By evening the circles are filled. Two of the three names have a house attached. The third is a girl called Pip Ash, and nobody has heard of her family because her family sweeps gutters.

Nobody in the pens is surprised. A couple of the old hands laugh about it. One of them stops laughing when he realises you are new to this one.

---

### The Night Before the Match

Snak comes into the pen with a bottle she does not offer to share and sits down heavy.

> "Right. This one's different, so listen."
>
> "Once a year they pull three kids out of a drum and put 'em in a box over the sand. Gilded thing, cushions, guards. Each kid gets a little panel with three brass buttons on it. Nine buttons total. Each one works once, and once it's gone it's gone."
>
> "The buttons do things to the *arena*. Not to a fighter — to the whole floor. Everyone on the sand at once, or whoever the thing happens to land on. The kids can't pick who. They can't aim. They press, and whatever it is happens to all of you, and to the crew you're fighting, same as you."

She takes a drink.

> "The buttons have little pictures stamped on 'em. A sun, a bell, that sort of thing. Means nothing — the magisters recut 'em every year so nobody can sell you last year's answers. When a kid presses one, the picture lights up on the front of the box so the whole crowd can see which one went. That's all you get. You'll know one's been used. You won't know what's coming till it's already on you."

> "Two things you need in your head. One: **you cannot get to that box**. It's thirty feet up and warded by the same stone that keeps the crowd from getting cooked. People have tried. It's a good way to look stupid and die tired."

> "Two: the kids are *kids*. Crowd loves 'em. You so much as scowl at that box where anyone can see and the mob will turn on you in about four seconds. Whatever happens out there, it is not the little girl's fault."

She pushes something across the table — a small chalk-white bead.

> "One each. Betting houses insisted, on account of a match nobody can predict is a match nobody can bet on. You hold it. When something's about to happen *to you* — a spell, one of the buttons, whatever — you crush it, and it doesn't. Just you. Everyone else still gets it."
>
> "Once. That's it. And don't get clever and save it — crowd counts. They see you walk off that sand still holding it, they'll call you a miser and you'll wear that for a season."

> "Oh — and they'll bring the kids down to the gate before the bout. Portraits, the oath, all that. You'll be standing right there with 'em. Sponsors watch who each kid takes a shine to, and the odds move on it. Make of that what you will."

She stands.

> "Your opposition's Bassa's crew. She's done this game four times. Her people have drilled for it — they know where to stand when the floor goes stupid. You haven't. That's the whole fight, really."

### If You Paid For Intel

Snak comes back later, and she is pleased with herself in a way that usually costs somebody something.

> "Got a fella. Cuts the stamps for the magisters. Drinks."

> "Two things worth what you paid."
>
> "First — one of them buttons kills the sound. All of it. Whole arena goes quiet as a grave, and it stays that way a good while. Anyone who does their work with words is going to have a bad minute. Plan for that one now, not when it lands. Work out how you'll tell each other things without saying them."

> "Second, and this is the one." She leans in. "That box gets its power from somewhere, doesn't it. Runs down the inside of the north wall — you can see the seam if you're looking, thin line of light in the stone. Goes all the way down to a brass plate behind the north gate. Ankle height. Nothing guarding it but the crew, and they only guard it because they're paid to."

> "You break that plate, that panel stops working. But my fella says there's a charge sitting in the line, and if you smash it, everything that panel had left goes off at once, right then, all of it, on top of you. If you've got soft hands and you take your time you can unpick it quiet instead."

> "Last thing, and I mean it. If you do that, you've taken the show away from ten thousand people who came to see it. They will hate you for it — *unless* you make it the best thing they see all year. There's no middle. Think hard."

### An Hour Before the Match

The armorers hand out the beads at the gate — one each, counted, logged in a ledger by a clerk from the betting houses who does not look up.

Anything rented from Snak's shop is fitted now. See [[snak_shop_session_10|Snak's Shop for Session 10]].

Then the children come down.

### As You Walk Into the Arena

> "TARSUS! It is that time of year again! Three names drawn from the drum, three hands on the brass, and NOT ONE OF THEM OVER ELEVEN YEARS OLD!"

> "Console the First — Vessia of House Marn-Doru, nine years old, and doesn't she look well!"

> "Console the Second — young Master Ottavan Sceld, ELEVEN, and his third year in the Box! A veteran, ladies and gentlemen, a VETERAN at eleven!"

> "And Console the Third — Pip Ash, eight years old, of... of Tanner's Row! TANNER'S ROW! The lottery is sworn, sealed, and utterly above suspicion!"

*(The crowd roars. It roars loudest for the third name, and it does not roar kindly, and it does not roar cruelly either. It just roars.)*

> "Nine buttons, my friends. Nine. Not one of them pressed yet. Let's find out what they DO!"
